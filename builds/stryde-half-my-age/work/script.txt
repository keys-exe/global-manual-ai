DAUGHTER (in the carriage, catching her breath, staring at her): “When did that happen?”
VO: “I’m 71 and I take the stairs faster than women half my age. Six weeks ago, I couldn’t.”

DAUGHTER (loaded with shopping bags, nodding at the lift): “Mum, the lift’s just there.”

DAUGHTER (entering, reflex): “Mum, wait, I’ll give you a…”

SCENE 2: ROCK BOTTOM

HER (calling down from the landing): “Just leave it by the stairs, love. I’ll get it later.”
HUSBAND (off, too quickly): “I’ll bring it up.”
VO: “Six weeks ago, I was going down my stairs backwards. One step at a time.”
HUSBAND: “Sleep alright?”
HER: “Fine, love.”
VO: “If I’m being honest, some days I wasn’t going down them at all. I’d just stay upstairs.”

SCENE 3: TRIED EVERYTHING + THE SUNDAY PLANT
VO: “The full knee brace I bought slipped down my leg. By evening, it was around my ankle. I tried everything. Physiotherapy. Painkillers. Cortisone injections. Every brace and sleeve on the market.”
VO (on the drawer closing): “Nothing worked. Nothing lasted. Nothing gave me my life back.”
HUSBAND (from the doorway, soft, watching her drop the brace in): “That drawer won’t shut soon.”
HER: “It shuts.”
SISTER (voice on the phone): “Sunday… shall we just do the garden centre? It’s all one level.”
HER: “Course, love. Easier.”
VO: “My sister’s knees are worse than mine. We planned our Sundays around one level.”

SCENE 4: THE WEDDING

VO: “Then my granddaughter got married in June. My cousin Barbara was there. She’s 74. And she was on the dance floor all night.”
HER (low, not taking her eyes off Barbara): “Her knees were worse than mine. Both of them. Bone on bone.”
HUSBAND: “Whose?”
HER: “Barbara’s.”
VO: “I always assumed hers weren’t as bad as mine. I always assumed mine were past the point of fixing.”

SCENE 5: BARBARA COMES TO STAY

VO: “Barbara came to stay the week after. She watched me work through my morning routine.”
BARBARA: “Can I show you something?”
VO: “A small black strap. Just below her kneecap.”
BARBARA (laying a second, boxed strap on the table): “They come in twos. I never used the spare.”

SCENE 6: THE SCEPTIC

VO: “It looked absurd. Too small.”
HER: “Barbara. This can’t possibly work on knees like mine.”
BARBARA: “Just put it on and walk down the stairs.”

SCENE 7: THE TEST

VO: “The first step, I didn’t have to hold the banister.”
VO: “Not the second. Not the third.”
VO: “All the way down. Both feet. Forwards.”
VO: “First step. Pain gone. Like a switch.”

SCENE 8: THE MECHANISM, FROM BARBARA

BARBARA: “Everything you’ve tried was designed to make you comfortable while your knee got worse. This one fixes why it hurts.”
BARBARA (two fingers below her own kneecap): “There’s one spot below the kneecap where every step lands. Every brace, every injection, every pill you’ve ever taken treated the whole knee. Not that spot.”
BARBARA: “That’s why nothing worked. The pain is pressure. Nothing more.”
BARBARA: “This sits on that spot and lifts the weight off.”

SCENE 9: PROOF IN THE FAMILY

VO: “Over 200,000 people wear one now. Sports doctors recommend it. Not the pharma companies. Sports doctors.”
VO: “Barbara’s husband wears one. He’s 76. He plays golf twice a week. Her niece wears one for tennis. She’s 22.”

SCENE 10: SIX WEEKS

VO: “I’ve worn mine every day for six weeks. Under my clothes. Nobody knows it’s there. No pills. No gel. No brace around my ankle by lunchtime.”
VO: “Yesterday I walked into town. Two miles there. Two miles back. I outwalked three women half my age.”
VO: “I stood in line for ten minutes without shifting my weight.”
CASHIER (reaching for the bags): “You alright carrying those, love?”
HER: “I am, actually.”
VO: “I carried two bags home. Nobody helped me.”

SCENE 11: THE HUSBAND

HUSBAND: “You were gone a long time.”
HER: “I know.”

SCENE 12: THE CAFE + REVEAL

VO: “That was six weeks of wearing the strap. Nothing else.”
VO: “I’m not the one everyone waits for anymore.”
FRIEND 1: “Right. What’s changed? And don’t say a haircut.”
HER: “Three weeks ago I couldn’t have crossed this room like that.”
FRIEND 2: “So what is it?”
HER (pulling the trouser leg up an inch): “It’s a strap.”
FRIEND 1: “That little thing?”
HER: “That’s what I said.”
VO: “So I’m just going to say it here. It’s called Stryde. Patellar Force Redirection. It’s been designed for three years with orthopaedic surgeons. It’s the real thing. Not the cheap knockoffs they sell on Amazon and fake Shopify sites.”

SCENE 13: THE OFFER, PLANTED IN THE STORY

VO: “Eleven years of knee pain. Gone the first step I took with it on.”
VO: “I bought a second pair for my sister the same week. She’s coming Sunday.” (cut to the front door ON the doorbell; the VO line announces the jump, no card)
