CONCEPT / IDEA — "YOU LOOK LIKE MY MOTHER" (public shaming + the friends · live-action movie · ~2:50)
At their thirtieth anniversary party, Susan's drunk husband Greg stands for a toast and it curdles, quietly, in front of thirty friends: "you look like my mother, when did that happen. Paula's the same age as you, ask her what she does." The attention grab is his cruelty. The deeper wound is what she overhears on the way out, her own friends agreeing she'd given up. She disappears for months. Then one friend, Beth, concedes the truth (yes, Paula had Botox) and pivots to the real secret, the Facelove stick, no needles, and does Susan's face before the next party. The payoff is Susan walking back into the same group she'd been avoiding, radiant, and Paula, the woman she was told to take notes from, crossing the room to ask HER what she's doing.
Problem to solution aware. Villain is the wrong formula and being counted out, never her age. Payoff belongs to Susan and the friend who helped her, not the husband. Cover, not erase.

The five things that make or break this cut
1. Warm toast, then a quiet verdict. "You look like my mother, when did that happen," said puzzled, not shouted. Push into HER face, never his, hold the frozen beat too long.
2. Both wounds up front. His public insult grabs, the friends' overheard whisper guts. His shout, then their whisper.
3. The Botox concede is the trust unlock. "Paula had Botox, of course she looks like that. I'm not saying you do that. Watch this." Agreeing with what she saw, then pivoting, is what sells the no-needles alternative.
4. A friend does her makeup. Beth's hands, the real colour change, the stick as a secret handed over, not a pitch.
5. Payoff rhymes the opening + the Paula boomerang. Same yard, opposite silence. The woman she was told to take notes from now asks HER. "Go enjoy the party, Greg." Villain is the formula and being counted out, never her age.
